# Festival — recommended decisions

Worked answers to the ten open questions in `FESTIVAL-SCOPE.md`, with the
reasoning behind each. These are recommendations, not locked decisions.
Override any of them; the reasoning is here so overriding is informed.

Constraints assumed throughout: solo operator, pre-launch, cost-sensitive,
first cycle with no track record. **The metric is entries.** Anything that
suppresses entry volume is working against the point.

---

## 1. Online, physical or hybrid → **Online**

A physical event buys prestige and press. A first-cycle festival has no
prestige to leverage, so you would be paying for something you cannot yet
spend. Venue, insurance, travel and logistics are real money against a
goal — entry volume — that a physical event does not serve. AI filmmakers
are already online-native, and online is global, which directly grows
entries.

**Do:** online submission, online screening, and a **live-streamed awards
announcement**. The stream costs almost nothing and creates the event
moment that makes it feel like a festival rather than a form.

**Revisit** a physical finale in cycle two or three, once the name carries
something.

---

## 2. FilmFreeway, direct, or both → **Both, direct-primary**

FilmFreeway is where filmmakers go looking for festivals. Not listing
makes you close to invisible to the people you want. But it takes a cut,
owns part of the relationship, and a brand-new festival with no reviews
ranks poorly there anyway.

The asymmetry that decides it: **you are building submission intake
regardless**, because Blackflix needs it. Direct submission therefore
costs almost nothing to operate, keeps the whole fee, and gives you the
entrant relationship outright.

**Do:** direct submission as the primary channel, priced lower.
FilmFreeway as a paid acquisition channel to reach filmmakers who would
never otherwise find you. Treat its fees as marketing spend, not overhead.

---

## 3. Judging → **Two tiers, and the jury is a marketing asset**

**Round one is a volume problem.** Three hundred entries is roughly fifty
hours of viewing. One person cannot, and unpaid enthusiasm does not
survive hour thirty.

**The final jury is a credibility problem.** Whose names are on it is what
makes the festival real to an entrant deciding whether to bother.

**Do:**
- **Round one:** paid screeners or a vetted community panel, working to a
  written rubric, each film seen by at least two people. Community
  screening is cheaper and builds investment, at the cost of calibration —
  which a rubric and overlap largely fixes.
- **Final jury:** three to five named people — Black filmmakers, critics,
  programmers, AI artists. Honorarium in the low hundreds each, or a
  strong comp plus credit.

**Announce the jury before entries close.** Their names sell entries, and
that is the metric.

---

## 4. Categories → **Three, and one of them markets for you**

Every category multiplies judging work and divides prestige. Genre
categories are also premature: you do not know the entry distribution
until you have one.

**Do:**
- **Best Film** — the headline
- **Best First-Time Creator** — directly grows entries by giving newcomers
  a reason to think they can place
- **Community Choice** — voted, not judged

Community Choice is the strategically valuable one. Entrants campaign
their own networks for votes, which markets the festival on their time
rather than your budget. It costs a voting mechanism and nothing else.

**Avoid** genre splits in cycle one. Add them in cycle two, informed by
what actually arrived.

---

## 5. Entry fee ladder → **Low, tiered by deadline**

Standard practice is early / regular / late, and it materially increases
revenue by rewarding early commitment. But high fees suppress volume, and
volume is the metric. A first-time festival also has no track record to
justify premium pricing.

**Do:**

| Deadline | Direct | Via FilmFreeway |
|---|---|---|
| Early | ~$15 | ~$20 |
| Regular | ~$25 | ~$30 |
| Late | ~$35 | ~$40 |

Direct priced below FilmFreeway pushes entrants to the channel you own.

**Consider** a reduced or waived fee for first-time creators. It costs
little, grows volume, and reinforces the category.

At three hundred entries averaging ~$25, that is roughly **$7,500 in
entry revenue**.

---

## 6. What disqualifies → **Publish this before entries open**

Draft grounds for disqualification:

- Not AI-generated, or fails the disclosure requirement
- Uses music, footage or assets the entrant has not cleared
- Uses a real person's likeness or voice without documented consent
- Hateful content, harassment, or any sexual content involving minors
- Not the entrant's own work
- Missing required disclosures or warranties

**Not disqualifying:** wrong file format, missing captions, technical
spec failures. Those get a fix-and-resubmit window. Disqualifying people
for a codec loses entries for no benefit.

---

## 7. AI provenance → **You cannot fully verify it. Design for that.**

Detection tools are unreliable in both directions. Any system claiming to
verify AI generation with confidence is overselling.

**Do — layered, honest:**
1. **Mandatory disclosure:** tools used, workflow, what was generated
   versus edited. A form, required to submit.
2. **Warranty and indemnity** in the submission agreement. This is legal
   protection, not verification — it moves liability to the submitter,
   which is the realistic goal.
3. **C2PA / Content Credentials** metadata where the entrant's tools
   support it. An emerging provenance standard; capture it when present,
   do not require it.
4. **Challenge process.** Entrants police each other better than any tool.
   Give them a route to dispute, and an investigation path.

**Say plainly in the rules** that verification is imperfect and that the
submitter carries responsibility. Overclaiming here is how festivals end
up in public arguments they cannot win.

---

## 8. Feedback to non-winners → **Paid add-on, not default**

Written feedback on three hundred entries is a crushing load, and
promising it then delivering late is worse than never offering.

**Do:** no individual feedback as standard, stated clearly up front.
Publish the rubric so entrants understand how they were assessed. Offer
written notes as a **paid add-on at submission** (~$10–15) — common
festival practice, generates revenue, and self-limits to the people who
actually want it.

---

## 9. Legal entity → **ACND INT. LLC. for cycle one — but get this checked**

A separate entity isolates festival liability from the platform. It also
costs money and administration you do not need at this scale. For cycle
one the exposure is manageable if the submission agreement carries proper
warranties and indemnities.

**Two things a lawyer should confirm before entries open:**

**Contest regulation.** An entry fee combined with prizes can engage
lottery and sweepstakes rules in some US states. Skill-based judged
competitions are the standard exemption — but the exemption depends on the
judging genuinely being skill-based and documented as such, which is
another reason the rubric matters.

**Prize tax.** Prizes are generally taxable income to US recipients, and
the payer may have reporting obligations. Comped memberships are still
value received.

This is the one question on the list where I would not act on my own
reasoning. Everything else here is judgement; this is law.

---

## 10. Budget ceiling → **Modelled, not decided**

Yours to set. The shape of it:

**Costs, cycle one**

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Final jury honoraria (5 × ~$300) | ~$1,500 |
| Round-one screeners (~50 hrs, if paid) | ~$1,000 (or $0 community) |
| Storage and bandwidth (~300 films on R2) | under $50 |
| FilmFreeway listing and fees | a few hundred |
| Promotion | $500–2,000 |
| Awards stream | minimal |
| Prize fulfilment (comped access) | opportunity cost, not cash |
| **Cash out** | **roughly $3,000–5,000** |

**Revenue** at 300 entries × ~$25 ≈ **$7,500**

**Break-even lands somewhere near 150–200 entries.** Below that the cycle
loses money; above it, the festival funds itself and the awareness is
free.

Storage is negligible — R2 charges no egress, and three hundred films is
well under a terabyte. The real costs are people and promotion.

---

## What this changes in the build

Nothing structural. Three implications worth carrying forward:

- **Community Choice voting** is a build item, small but real
- **Feedback as a paid add-on** means the submission flow needs optional
  paid extras, which is Stripe work in core
- **Direct-cheaper-than-FilmFreeway** means the intake must handle two
  price paths
